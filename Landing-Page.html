Answers to Common Trademark & Patent Questions

Our experts are available to assist you via phone or chat.

A trademark functions as a legal shield for the unique identity of a particular business or product, including its name, logo, and symbols. This safeguard extends to diverse components such as business names, symbols, logos, sounds, and even particular colors associated with a brand. Once registered, the trademark owner gains the right to display the ® symbol alongside their name, logo, or slogan.

Unique symbols refer to visually distinct and recognizable identifiers associated with a particular brand or product. Examples include the recognizable orange color of Reese's peanut butter cup packaging and the iconic bitten fruit symbol representing Apple computers. These symbols are trademarked and serve as immediate triggers for brand and product recognition.

Registering a trademark holds paramount importance as it provides legal protection for a brand nationwide, guarding against counterfeit and fraudulent practices. It enables the brand to pursue legal recourse against competitors attempting to imitate its concepts. Obtaining a trademark bestows exclusive rights to the brand for its names, slogans, and logos, mitigating costs associated with rebranding efforts.

Moreover, displaying the ® symbol following trademark registration serves as a visible indication of the brand's protection and credibility. Trademarks endure indefinitely as long as they are actively used, with regular submission of maintenance documents ensuring their continuous validity.

While some may believe that obtaining a trademark is solely a concern for large corporations, protecting the fruits of your labor and preserving your brand identity holds equal importance for small businesses. Small enterprises can trademark not only their brand logos but also words, symbols, packaging, and even unique sounds. Determining if a specific aspect of your brand identity qualifies for trademark protection requires a case-by-case assessment, and seeking guidance from a small business intellectual property attorney can be advantageous in this regard.

Experts advise small businesses to strive for maximum uniqueness in their logos and other branding elements. This approach offers two benefits: ensuring immediate brand recognition and bolstering protection for your intellectual property by making it more difficult for others to replicate.
